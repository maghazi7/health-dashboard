## 1. Your Calorie Targets

> [!warning] A note on online calorie calculators
> Generic online calculators are notoriously inaccurate. They use broad activity level dropdowns that don't capture real life well. The numbers below are a solid **starting point**, but the most accurate way to find your true maintenance is to track your food intake for 2 weeks without changing anything — if your weight stays stable, that intake is your real maintenance. No calculator beats real-world data from your own body.

### BMR — Basal Metabolic Rate (Mifflin-St Jeor)

Formula for men: **(10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5**

```
Weight:  185 lbs = 83.9 kg
Height:  5'6" = 167.6 cm
Age:     27

BMR = (10 × 83.9) + (6.25 × 167.6) − (5 × 27) + 5
    = 839 + 1,047.5 − 135 + 5
    = 1,757 kcal
```

This is what your body burns just existing — breathing, pumping blood, keeping your brain running. You'd burn this if you stayed in bed all day.

### TDEE — Total Daily Energy Expenditure

Your activity multiplier depends on your **job + exercise combined**:

| Scenario | Multiplier | TDEE |
|---|---|---|
| **Right now** (desk job, no gym) — Sedentary | × 1.2 | **2,108 kcal** |
| **With training** (desk job + 3–4 gym sessions/week) — Lightly active | × 1.375 | **2,416 kcal** |
| **Heavy training** (desk job + 5–6 sessions/week) — Moderately active | × 1.55 | **2,723 kcal** |

### Your Deficit Targets

We're subtracting 500 kcal from TDEE — this puts you at roughly 1 lb of fat loss per week from diet alone. On retatrutide, you'll likely lose faster initially (water weight + enhanced fat oxidation), but we **never** want to go below 1,400 kcal consistently — that's where muscle loss accelerates and metabolic adaptation kicks in.

| Phase | Target Calories |
|---|---|
| **Now (before gym restarts)** | **~1,600 kcal** |
| **Once training 3–4x/week (use this plan)** | **~1,900 kcal** |

> [!important] Retatrutide reality check
> Your biggest risk isn't eating too much — it's eating **too little**. Retatrutide will crush your appetite. Some days you won't feel like eating at all. On those days, **protein is non-negotiable**. If you can only manage two meals, make them both protein-heavy. A protein shake counts. Muscle loss on GLP-1 agonists is real and documented — the gym + high protein are your insurance policy.

## 5. Your Personal Fat Loss Rules

These are specific to you, Amir — not generic advice.

### Rule 1: Protein is the only non-negotiable
On retatrutide, your appetite will be unpredictable. Some days you'll eat 3 meals, some days you'll barely manage 2. That's fine — but wherever your calories land, **160g of protein must happen.** If you're not hungry for a full meal, drink a shake. If you skip lunch, make dinner double-protein. Everything else is flexible. Protein is not.

### Rule 2: The Häagen-Dazs is on Saturday
You love it. It's in the plan. But it's a **Saturday ritual**, not a nightly habit. Buy the mini cups, not the pint. On other nights when the craving hits, frozen yogurt bark or a protein ice cream. This isn't about willpower — it's about removing the decision by having a rule.

### Rule 3: Sunday prep or the week falls apart
You're an engineer — you know systems beat willpower. Two hours on Sunday cooking chicken tikka, rice, and prepped veggies means weeknight dinners take 10 minutes to assemble. Skip the prep, and by Wednesday you're ordering Uber Eats and blowing past your targets. **The prep IS the plan.**

### Rule 4: A protein shake is a meal on low-appetite days
Retatrutide will have days where food sounds terrible. Don't skip eating — chug a shake. 30g of protein, 120 calories, takes 30 seconds. It's not ideal, but it's infinitely better than eating nothing and losing muscle. Keep whey protein at your desk at work too.

### Rule 5: Train or lose muscle — there's no middle ground
GLP-1 agonists don't just burn fat — they can burn muscle too, especially without resistance training. Getting back in the gym isn't optional on this protocol. **3–4 days of lifting per week minimum.** The cardio is a bonus. The lifting is survival for your muscle mass during this cut.

## 7. Hydration Target

### Your daily target: 3–3.5 litres (roughly 100–120 oz)

**Calculation:**
```
Base: 35ml × 83.9 kg = 2,937 ml (~3.0L)
Exercise days: + 500ml per hour of training
```

That puts you at **3.0L on rest days**, **3.5L on training days**.

### Why hydration matters for fat loss

This isn't filler advice — hydration directly impacts your cut:

- **Hunger signal confusion**: Your brain often misreads thirst as hunger. On retatrutide, your hunger signals are already altered — proper hydration helps you distinguish real hunger from dehydration. This matters for hitting your macros intentionally, not just eating because your body is confused.
- **Metabolic function**: Your liver and kidneys need water to process fat metabolism. Dehydrated = sluggish fat burning. Studies show even mild dehydration (2%) reduces metabolic rate.
- **Gym performance**: 1–2% dehydration drops strength by up to 10%. You're trying to preserve muscle — don't handicap your workouts by showing up dehydrated.
- **Energy and focus**: You're at a desk all day doing precision engineering work. Dehydration tanks cognitive function before you even feel thirsty. Keep a bottle at your desk.

### 4 practical tips for you specifically

1. **Get a 1L bottle for your desk at Apple.** Fill it when you arrive, finish it by lunch, fill it again, finish by end of day. That's 2L done without thinking. Mark times on the bottle if it helps.
2. **Drink 500ml first thing in the morning** before coffee or food. After 6–7 hours of sleep (especially with mouth breathing from the sleep apnea recovery), you wake up dehydrated. This kickstarts everything.
3. **Flavour it if plain water bores you.** Cucumber + mint, lemon slices, or zero-calorie electrolyte packets (LMNT, Liquid IV sugar-free). Whatever makes you actually drink it.
4. **On training days, bring a separate gym bottle (750ml).** Sip throughout the workout. This is in addition to your desk intake, not instead of it.

## 8. Supplement Recommendations

> [!warning] The 1% rule
> Supplements are the 1%. Food, training, sleep, and consistency are the 99%. Nothing in this section will work if the meal plan and gym time don't happen first. That said, these specific supplements are genuinely evidence-backed and relevant to YOUR situation.

### Whey Protein — ⭐ Top Priority

- **Dose**: 1 scoop (25–30g protein) daily
- **When**: Post-workout, or anytime you need to close a protein gap
- **Why for you**: On retatrutide, your appetite is suppressed. You told me hitting protein is the hard part. A shake is the fastest, easiest way to get 30g of protein when food sounds unappealing. This is your #1 supplement by far.
- **Budget pick**: Optimum Nutrition Gold Standard Whey (~$30/2lb) or MyProtein Impact Whey (~$20/2.2lb on sale). Both mix well and taste decent.

### Creatine Monohydrate — ⭐ Essential

- **Dose**: 5g daily (no loading phase needed)
- **When**: Any time — morning, post-workout, with a meal. Timing doesn't matter, consistency does.
- **Why for you**: Creatine is the most researched sports supplement in history. It increases strength output (you'll lift more → build/preserve more muscle), improves body composition, and has cognitive benefits (relevant for your desk work). It also helps with hydration at the cellular level. On a cut with retatrutide, creatine helps you hold onto muscle you'd otherwise lose. There is zero downside.
- **Budget pick**: Any unflavoured creatine monohydrate. Bulk Supplements or Nutricost on Amazon (~$15 for 6+ months). Don't pay for fancy versions — monohydrate is the only form with strong evidence.
- **Note**: You may gain 2–3 lbs of water weight initially. This is intracellular water (in your muscles, not bloating). Ignore it on the scale — it's a good thing.

### Vitamin D3 — Important

- **Dose**: 4,000–5,000 IU daily
- **When**: With a meal containing fat (it's fat-soluble)
- **Why for you**: You work a desk job indoors in Texas. Even with Texas sun, office workers are commonly deficient. Vitamin D supports immune function, mood (relevant with your moderate stress levels), testosterone production, and sleep quality — all things you need right now. Get your levels tested if you haven't recently.
- **Budget pick**: Nature's Bounty or Kirkland (Costco) D3 5000 IU — under $10 for a year's supply.

### Omega-3 Fish Oil

- **Dose**: 2–3g combined EPA/DHA daily (check the label — you want EPA+DHA totals, not just "fish oil" weight)
- **When**: With a meal
- **Why for you**: Anti-inflammatory, joint health (important as you ramp back into training), and recovery support. Also supports brain function — useful when you're on a caloric deficit and your body is under stress.
- **Budget pick**: Kirkland Signature Fish Oil or Nordic Naturals Ultimate Omega. Look for third-party tested (IFOS certified) to avoid heavy metals.

### Magnesium Glycinate — For Your Sleep

- **Dose**: 300–400mg magnesium glycinate before bed
- **When**: 30–60 minutes before sleep
- **Why for you**: You specifically said your sleep is bad — 6–7 hours with sleep apnea. Magnesium glycinate is the best-absorbed form and has calming properties. It won't fix sleep apnea, but it can improve sleep quality within the hours you do get. Better sleep = better recovery = better fat loss = better gym performance. It's a multiplier.
- **Budget pick**: Doctor's Best Magnesium Glycinate (~$15 for 120 caps) or Nature Made.

### Caffeine — Strategic Use

- **Dose**: 100–200mg pre-workout (roughly 1–2 cups of coffee)
- **When**: 30 minutes before training. Avoid after 2 PM to protect sleep.
- **Why for you**: If you train in the morning or after work, caffeine improves workout performance, strength output, and fat oxidation. But given your sleep issues, timing is critical — no caffeine in the afternoon or evening. If you're sensitive to caffeine or it disrupts your sleep at all, skip this one.
- **Budget pick**: Regular black coffee. Done.
